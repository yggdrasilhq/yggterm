## SECTION 1 — YOUR JOB: THE SETTLE WINDOW (fix (a))

Seven agent sessions were destroyed on 2026-08-13 by a two-second window, and
**routed to this seat by 6.0 as the most valuable thing on the campaign.**

    22:31:40  predecessor  pty_fd_handoff_sweep  "AllMoved { moved: 38 }"
    22:31:42  successor    spawned_daemon_exit    signal 15 (SIGTERM)

`hand_off_all_runtimes` is all-or-nothing and the predecessor then **exits**,
because exiting is the only way to release its own copies of the masters. So it
releases on the successor's **acceptance**, never on its **survival**. Kill a
young successor and every session on the host dies with no process holding
anything.

⛔⛔ **THE HAZARD THAT MUST BE PRICED BEFORE YOU BUILD, AND IT IS WHY I DID NOT
START IT AT 64% CONTEXT.** After the sweep, **both** processes hold the master
fds and the predecessor's reader threads are still attached. The existing 250 ms
sleep before exit is already a double-read window; **a 10 s settle interval is a
10 s double-read window**, and the code's own comment says two daemons reading
one PTY means the shell never sees EOF. ⇒ **too generous fails differently from
too eager, and neither is obviously cheaper than seven agents' work.**

⭐ **The design to test first:** separate *holding the descriptor* from *serving
the session* — the predecessor **stops reading** but keeps the fds open, then
exits once the successor is confirmed alive. The current code conflates the two
because exiting was always both at once.
⛔ **Prove it in a sandbox** (§4), never by deploying and watching. You now have
the cost of getting it wrong measured at seven agents' work.

  shipped a commit over a failing protocol-stamp test this way. Redirect to a
  file, check `$?`, then grep.
- ⛔ **`live_sessions[]` is keyed by ROW path (`cc-runtime://…`);
