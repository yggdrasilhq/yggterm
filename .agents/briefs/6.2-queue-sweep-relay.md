## ⭐ THE METHOD, AND IT IS THE WHOLE OF THE VALUE

**Make the instrument say the OTHER thing.** Never re-read the field that filed
the entry. Four laws, every one of them learned by being caught out tonight:

- ⛔ **BOTH CONTROLS, SAME RUN.** Prove the instrument can say DIRTY before
  trusting a CLEAN, and CLEAN before trusting a DIRTY.
- ⛔ **A measurement taken in the only condition that cannot discriminate is not a
  weak measurement, it is NO measurement — and it feels exactly like a strong
  one.** It happened four times in one night. A `changed:false` over a sorted
  list, a `0 orphans` with zero surfaces in existence, a start-page call made
  while already on the start page.
- ⛔ **A VERIFICATION HARNESS NEEDS ITS OWN POSITIVE CONTROL.** A read-back that
  interpolated a session path through two shells matched nothing and reported a
  row missing that was plainly there — a check that FAILS OPEN and reads exactly
  like a finding.
- ⛔ **TEST A STATE-CHANGING VERB FROM A STATE IT WOULD HAVE TO CHANGE**, and
  restore the operator's view immediately afterwards.

**Three outcomes are all wins, and say which one you reached:** retract · confirm
with the measurement inline · narrow. ⭐ **"Smaller than filed" is as useful as
"wrong".** And **recording that you cleared an entry BY INSPECTION is a
deliverable** — it stops the next sweeper spending the same hour.

## INSTRUMENT NOTES THAT WILL SAVE YOU AN HOUR

- **`live_member` is not a liveness flag** — every row carries it, including ones
  with no running CLI. It means "member of the Live group".
- **`server app start-page` NAVIGATES.** It moves the operator's view.
- **`pgrep -f <pattern>` matches your own command line.** Build the pattern at
  runtime or invoke through a script file, or you will diagnose your own shell.
- **`server terminal screen` is on the `yggterm` binary, not `yggterm-headless`**,
  which answers `unsupported server command: terminal`.
- **`capture_backend_attempts`**, not `capture_attempts`.
- **Ask the BUILD, not the version.** A version covers a RANGE of commits — two
  builds legitimately wear one number, and `server daemons`' BUILD column is what
  separates them.
